# Boosting Neural Combinatorial Optimization for Large-Scale Vehicle Routing Problems

> **TL;DR:** SIL strengthens large-scale constructive routing through self-improved learning and partial-route reconstruction from solutions generated by the current model.

## Motivation

Supervised large-scale labels are costly and small-scale training alone leaves a substantial distribution gap.

## Contributions

- Introduces **SIL** as a concrete neural routing method for Traveling Salesman Problem and Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem.
- The model generates candidate routes, extracts improved partial-solution supervision, and retrains a light-encoder/heavy-decoder policy on its own stronger targets.
- Evaluates solution quality together with the inference or search budget needed to obtain it.

## Methodology

1. The instance and current solution state are represented for Traveling Salesman Problem and Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem.
2. The model generates candidate routes, extracts improved partial-solution supervision, and retrains a light-encoder/heavy-decoder policy on its own stronger targets.
3. Greedy construction and partial reconstruction are separate inference modes; reconstruction rounds add a search budget.

## Experiments

- **Problems:** Traveling Salesman Problem; Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem.
- **Scale and budget:** The study evaluates TSP and CVRP from standard training sizes through 1,000-node synthetic and public instances.
- **Metrics:** Objective value or optimality gap, runtime, and—where relevant—generalization across scale, distribution, or constraint variants.
- **Main finding:** Self-improved targets and reconstruction materially improve large-scale generalization over the starting constructive policy.

## Limitations

### Reported by the Authors

- Iterative data generation is expensive and the strongest reconstruction results are not one-pass inference.

### Curator Notes

- Results should be compared only after matching trajectory count, augmentation, adaptation steps, local-search iterations, and hardware assumptions.
- The repository scope label reflects whether one trained checkpoint covers distinct optimization problems, not whether the algorithmic framework is reusable.
